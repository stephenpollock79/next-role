How to use

Sites the sweep can't read

Some job sites need a login, block automated reading, or only show jobs to a real browser. LinkedIn is the usual example. The sweep can't read them, but it can read their job-alert emails.

  1. Sign up to the site yourself. Claude won't create accounts or enter passwords. 5 min a site
  2. Ask Claude to set up the alerts. On desktop, with Claude in Chrome and you signed in, Claude creates the saved searches from your search terms and the places you'd work, as often as the site allows.
  3. Ask Claude to set up the email filter. One Gmail filter labels every alert Job Alerts and skips the inbox. Claude adds each new sender to it, with your OK.
  4. Add it as a source. Under Alert emails in Sourcing › Auto sweep, so its finds are counted.
  5. Check the first alert lands. next day If nothing with listings arrives within two days, the signup didn't take. Mark the source Trial until it does.

An alert tells the sweep a role exists. It still checks the employer's own site before counting it as live.

How the board works: one loop

Five stages. The first three run every week; the last two are how the search gets better.

1

You — what you want

About meset up once, then as needed
Claude does
  • Drafts your profile, skills and criteria at setup.
  • Reads them in every sweep, every piece of research, every CV and every prep.
You do
  • Keep My criteria true: deal-breakers, preferences, pluses, concerns and search terms. Each is its own row; changes are live from the next sweep.
  • Upload your CV and cover letter in My CV & letter; check your top 20 in Skills.
2

Find — roles come in

Sourcingtwice a week, plus your own days
Claude does
  • The auto sweep runs twice a week on its scheduled task: alert emails, job boards, your target companies' careers pages and your search terms.
  • Applies your deal-breakers, gives each role a rough Role Fit and fills the Longlist. What it cut is logged in Sourcing › Auto sweep.
  • Warns you on Overview and in Data health if a sweep is overdue, worked out from when sweeps usually run.
You do
  • Check the sites in Manual (Sites I check, Warm routes) on the days your repeating tasks in Week ahead say.
  • Add what you find with + New › Add roles; a dry search is one tap on Nothing new.
  • Sites that need a login: see Set up › Sites the sweep can't read.
  • Now and then, upload your LinkedIn connections to Warm routes and add the companies where you know someone; rating them Want it or Would consider puts their job boards in the sweep.
3

Decide, apply, interview

Roles · Week aheadevery day or two
Claude does
  • Each morning: checks every open ad is still live (closing longlist roles that have gone), then researches new Shortlist roles and scores their Role Fit.
  • At To apply: a CV and cover letter drafted as tracked changes.
  • When you apply: screen prep within a day (your story, why them, traps, salary, questions to ask). After the screen: prep for the next round only, from the job ad, the CV you sent, the company's interview materials and your notes. It shows on the meeting as Advice for this one.
  • When you ask: a one-page cheat sheet for a round, built from that prep, your notes, your latest mock and your starred questions.
You do
  • Longlist, on your review days (for example Mon and Thu, the day after each sweep): press Mass review, a minute a role. Score Fun and Shot, then send on or reject.
  • Shortlist, the morning after its research (for example Tue and Fri; until then it shows Queued for research): decide to apply, park or reject. If a link couldn't be read, paste the ad on the card.
  • To apply: treat the drafts as a start. Iterate, send, upload the finals and mark Applied.
  • Applied: add each interview on the role's Interviews tab, plus any interview guide or case brief the company sends (Add material). Star the questions you must ask. Log every conversation so the next prep uses it.
  • Before a round: read the prep, press Practise for a mock interview against it, then Make cheat sheet and download the PDF to keep open during the call.
  • The Roles badge and Overview's Waiting on you show what's on you.
4

Learn — see what's working

Insightsa glance each week
Claude does
  • Works out the highlights: where roles drop out, which channels lead somewhere, your application pace and mix.
  • Suggest a change on a highlight drafts the fix for you to accept.
You do
  • Glance at the Highlights on Overview. Open Insights when something looks off: filter, group by channel or fit, zoom into one stage.
5

Tune — fix where it's stuck

Search reviewwhen it stalls — no fixed schedule
Claude does
  • Reads the whole loop and says where it's stuck: finding, deciding, applying or interviews. Not everything is a search problem.
  • Suggests changes to your setup, or things for you to do, with the numbers behind each.
You do
  • Talk it through: answer its question, then keep, change, turn down (with a reason) or ask about each suggestion. Nothing changes until you press Apply.
  • By hand: in My criteria, open a deal-breaker to see what it cut and what you turned down later; spot-check its cuts. Trial or drop sources in Sourcing.
  • Every change is in the change log with Undo. Want a reminder? Add a repeating task in Week ahead.

Good moments: after about ten applications, when the longlist feels thin or full of the wrong roles, or when applications stop turning into interviews.

Working with Claude

  • Change the board by asking. To add, change or remove a feature, or fix a bug, just ask Claude.
  • Big or messy updates: tell Claude, don't fill in forms. Dictate it as it comes, e.g. “I've set up two new meetings: a screen with Acme on Tuesday at 2 with their talent lead, and a coffee with Sam from Foo on Thursday”, and ask Claude to update the board.
  • Same for notes after a call, a recruiter's email, or a pile of links. Paste or dictate it and Claude puts each fact in the right place.
  • The forms here are for quick, single changes — a score, a status, one note.

About me

Sourcing

Sweeps run on their schedule, and on demand

No sourcing sweep has run yet. Every role on this board arrived by hand. A verification pass is not a sweep — it checks rows that already exist and cannot find anything new, so it is counted separately and never reported as “last run”.

How the queue is built

Sorted by each rule in turn. A later rule only breaks ties in the one before.

  1. Closing within seven days. The only key where waiting destroys the option.
  2. Warm route. Most likely of anything to produce a first conversation.
  3. Company priority. Want it first, then Would consider, then If it appears. Your appetite, not whether a role is open — that is derived from the board. Replaced target-list tier on 28 Sep.
  4. Fewest weak bands, then most strong bands.
  5. Newest first.

The batch is thirty. Everything below stays queued, not rejected. The cut is about capacity, not quality.

Mock interview

Practise a round with an AI interviewer that knows the role, then get short feedback on what to keep and what to change.

Tip: answer out loud with a dictation tool (e.g. Wispr Flow, or your computer's built-in dictation) and paste it in. It's closer to the real thing, and dictation spelling slips are ignored.

How to use it
  • Pick the role and up to 3 round types. The next booked round's types are preselected.
  • Focus drafts itself from your board (round prep, Role Fit gaps, last round's notes). Edit it, or use Sharpen with Claude.
  • Take-home: paste or upload the brief you were sent, then present it and take the panel's questions. Live case: paste how the invite says the exercise runs, and the interviewer writes a mock case that fits.
  • The interviewer reads the role's research, the company's interview process, your notes, profile and CV (the one you sent, else your base CV), then asks one question at a time.
  • At the end you get a two-line headline, what to keep doing and what to improve, and, folded underneath, every answer rated Good, Average or Fix, plus feedback on your questions with sharper ones to ask. No score and no pass or fail.
  • You're asked whether to mark the next round's prep as done. Runs on your own Claude usage.
